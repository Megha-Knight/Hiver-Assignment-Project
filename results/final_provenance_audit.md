## 1. Discovered Provenance Issues

During the final pre-submission audit of the evaluation pipeline and benchmark artifacts, the following provenance issues were identified and reconciled:

1. **200-Example Golden Benchmark Misattribution**:
   - *Original Claim*: Documentation and artifact headers described `data/golden/amazonhelp_golden_v1_human_validated.jsonl` as "200 human-validated checkpoints" with metadata fields indicating `human_review_status = "REVIEWED"` and `annotator_id = "human_expert_annotator_1"`.
   - *Actual Execution*: The 200 checkpoints were sampled using fixed seed 42 from the Validation partition, initially pre-annotated via keyword heuristics, and subsequently adjudicated by an automated rule-based Python script (`scripts/expert_review_adjudicator.py`) applying domain precedence rules. No manual turn-by-turn human verification occurred for the full 200 checkpoints.
   - *Remediation*: The benchmark was formally reclassified across the entire repository as the **200-example rule-based pre-annotated evaluation benchmark**. Metadata fields were updated to `human_review_status = "NOT_HUMAN_REVIEWED"` and `annotator_id = "rule_adjudicator_v1"`.

2. **Phase 7D N=40 Response-Quality Audit Misattribution**:
   - *Original Claim*: Reports in `results/phase7/` described the 40 response-quality ratings as "expert human reviews" and computed "inter-annotator agreement" between human and LLM judge.
   - *Actual Execution*: The ratings in `data/evaluation/human_reviews_n40.jsonl` were produced by an automated rule-heuristic script (`scripts/conduct_expert_reviews.py`) applying pattern matching to generated texts.
   - *Remediation*: The ratings were reclassified as an **automated rule-based heuristic response audit**. Records were marked with `review_status = "NOT_HUMAN_REVIEWED"`, `reviewer_id = "heuristic_rule_adjudicator"`, and `review_provenance = "automated_heuristic_rule_audit"`. The statistical comparison was renamed from "human agreement" to **heuristic-to-LLM judge calibration**.

3. **Same-Family LLM Judge Framing**:
   - *Original Claim*: In some early documents, the `llama3.2:1b` judge was referenced without prominent disclosure of same-family leniency or mode collapse.
   - *Remediation*: The judge is consistently described as a **same-family local LLM judge**, with explicit disclosure of its +0.36 leniency bias over heuristic scores and its tendency toward mode collapse around integer 4.0.

4. **Automated Evidence Grounding Framing**:
   - *Original Claim*: The 99.50% grounding metric was previously susceptible to being interpreted as independent factual verification.
   - *Remediation*: The evaluator is explicitly described as an **automated evidence-alignment evaluator**, verifying absence of unsupported policy commitments and fabricated order/tracking numbers against retrieved exemplars.

## 7. Remaining Limitations

1. **Benchmark Ground-Truth Grounding**:
   - Because the 200-example benchmark reflects rule-based pre-annotation rather than human consensus, subtle conversational nuances where customer intent is ambiguous may reflect heuristic heuristics rather than actual customer expectations.
2. **Evaluator Mode Collapse**:
   - The small local open-weight judge (`llama3.2:1b`) exhibits severe mode collapse, clustering around integer 4.0/5.0 and resulting in zero judge variance on several rubric dimensions.
3. **Escalation False Auto-Handle Rate (66.67%)**:
   - The hybrid agent captured only 15 of 45 escalation-worthy checkpoints, representing an operational risk where autonomous handling is attempted on complex inquiries.
4. **Hardware Latency Boundary**:
   - While commodity CPU execution is functional (~1.66s/turn), high-throughput production deployment requires GPU infrastructure with continuous batching (vLLM/Triton).
